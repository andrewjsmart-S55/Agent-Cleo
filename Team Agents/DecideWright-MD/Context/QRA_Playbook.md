# **Quantitative Risk Analysis (QRA) Playbook**

## **Overview**

The **Quantitative Risk Analysis (QRA) Playbook** from DecideWright provides a practical, phased roadmap for transforming traditional, qualitative risk management into a data-driven, quantitative discipline. It enables organizations to move from subjective color-coded matrices to measurable insights that drive better decisions, resource allocation, and resilience.

The Playbook is structured around three distinct phases — **Pilot**, **Change**, and **Transform** — each designed to demonstrate increasing value and organizational maturity.

## **1. The Case for Quantitative Risk Analysis**

### **1.1 The Problem with Qualitative Risk Management**
Qualitative risk management methods rely on human judgment and subjective probability estimates. They use traffic light systems and heat maps that:
- Encourage bias and inconsistency
- Hide the magnitude of financial and operational exposure
- Prevent accurate comparison or prioritization of risks
- Create a false sense of confidence

### **1.2 The Quantitative Advantage**
Quantitative Risk Analysis replaces opinions with data. It enables:
- **Numerical understanding of exposure:** e.g., Opex at Risk, Capex at Risk, Revenue at Risk.
- **Model-based decision support:** Use of probability distributions, simulations, and sensitivity analysis.
- **Integration with performance metrics:** Align risk and reward, connect to OKRs or Balanced Scorecards.

## **2. The Three-Phase Playbook**

### **Phase 1: Pilot (Proof of Value)**
**Objective:** Provide a rapid, high-impact demonstration of the benefits of quantitative analysis.

**Activities:**
- Identify 3–5 high-priority risks (operational, cyber, or third-party)
- Quantify using DecideWright’s Rapid Risk Intelligence Framework
- Estimate distributions for likelihood and impact
- Perform sensitivity and Monte Carlo simulations
- Compare against existing qualitative matrices

**Deliverables:**
- Rapid Risk Intelligence Assessment Report
- Visualization: cost-of-exposure vs probability curve
- Management briefing comparing color-coded matrices vs quantitative outputs

**Outcomes:**
- Clear proof of value
- Executive engagement
- Foundation for departmental adoption

---

### **Phase 2: Change (Transform a Department)**
**Objective:** Institutionalize quantitative methods within a single domain.

**Activities:**
- Develop structured data models for risk, controls, and metrics
- Introduce time-driven activity-based costing (TDABC)
- Train analysts and managers in quantitative reasoning
- Embed simulation and analytics tools into workflows
- Create dashboards with real-time risk intelligence

**Deliverables:**
- Departmental QRA Data Model (in Supabase or similar)
- Live Quantitative Risk Dashboard
- Capability training and playbook materials

**Outcomes:**
- Repeatable, evidence-based decision-making
- Improved risk prioritization and investment planning
- Departmental proof of ROI

---

### **Phase 3: Transform (Enterprise Adoption)**
**Objective:** Expand quantitative risk management across the enterprise.

**Activities:**
- Establish enterprise data pipelines linking risks, controls, metrics, and business outcomes
- Integrate QRA outputs with strategic planning, capital allocation, and OKR systems
- Model enterprise exposure under different risk scenarios
- Develop enterprise dashboards for CRO, CFO, and board reporting

**Deliverables:**
- Enterprise Risk Intelligence Platform
- Strategic Risk-Performance Maps
- Integrated Board Reporting Dashboards

**Outcomes:**
- Enterprise-wide quantitative risk culture
- Strategic alignment of risk and performance
- Enhanced resilience and confidence in decision-making

## **3. Quantitative Metrics Framework**

| **Category** | **Metric Examples** | **Purpose** |
|---------------|---------------------|--------------|
| **Cost of Recovery** | Opex at Risk, Capex at Risk, Productivity Days at Risk | Quantify financial impact of incidents |
| **Business Outcomes Impact** | Organic Revenue at Risk, New Revenue at Risk, Service Disruption | Link risk exposure to business performance |
| **Control Effectiveness** | Risk Reduction Ratio, Control Confidence Index | Assess strength of control environment |
| **Resilience** | Mean Time to Recovery, Scenario Loss Distribution | Measure readiness and ability to recover |
